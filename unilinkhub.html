Project Overview

UniLinkHub is a student-focused digital marketplace intended to support student entrepreneurs and make it easier for students to discover businesses, products and services within their university communities.

Project purpose

The purpose of UniLinkHub is to provide student-owned businesses with a platform to promote their offerings, share business information and connect with potential customers.

My role

I was part of the Developer, Analyst and Tester team. My responsibilities included supporting sprint and backlog activities, data discovery and exploration, data validation and quality, and data analysis and updates.

My project involvement also included activities associated with business research, marketing strategy, proposal work and presentation preparation.

Agile approach

The team used an Agile approach to organise project work into sprints, maintain a product backlog, break larger activities into smaller tasks and monitor progress through Trello. Microsoft Teams, WhatsApp and Outlook supported team communication and coordination.

Definition of Done

Work is considered complete when the code has been completed and reviewed, tests have passed, relevant documentation has been updated, the Product Owner has accepted the work, deployment has been confirmed and the platform can represent client businesses and services.

Risks, dependencies and constraints

  • Dependencies: Internet access, equipment, database software, application programs and transport.
  • Constraints: Time, budget accuracy, shared tools and the team's familiarity with software.

Individual Reflection — Agile Team Charter

Developing the Agile Team Charter gave me a better understanding of the importance of structure, accountability and shared expectations when working as part of a team. Before work can progress effectively, everyone needs to understand the purpose of the project, their responsibilities and how their contributions fit into the bigger picture.

One of the key things I took away from this process was that collaboration requires more than simply dividing tasks between people. It requires communication, consistency and a willingness to take responsibility for the work assigned to you. Having clearly defined roles and working agreements helped establish a common direction for the team.

As a Developer, Analyst and Tester, I understood that my contribution extended beyond completing individual tasks. Supporting sprint activities, working with data and contributing to the overall project required me to remain aware of how my work affected the team's progress.

Overall, this experience reinforced the importance of being accountable, communicating effectively and understanding that the success of a team project depends on everyone's willingness to contribute towards a shared objective.

2. User Stories and Tasks

User stories describe the needs of people who will use UniLinkHub. They help the team understand the value behind a feature before breaking the work into smaller and more manageable tasks.

Illustrative product user stories

The following are example user stories derived from the planned product functionality. They should be checked against the team's original requirements.

US01 — Student registration

As a student, I want to create an account so that I can access the UniLinkHub platform.

Example tasks: Design the registration form, validate user details, implement account creation and test registration.

US02 — Business profile

As a student entrepreneur, I want to create a business profile so that customers can discover my business.

Example tasks: Capture business information, create the profile interface, validate submitted details and test profile creation.

US03 — Product and service listings

As a seller, I want to advertise products and services so that other students can view what my business offers.

Example tasks: Create listing functionality, capture product details, implement listing management and test listing updates.

US04 — Browse and search

As a buyer, I want to browse and search for products and services so that I can find what I need.

Example tasks: Create the browse interface, implement search and category filters, display matching listings and test search results.

US05 — Reporting and administration

As a platform administrator, I want to review reported content so that I can help maintain a safe and reliable marketplace.

Example tasks: Create a reporting mechanism, record reports, provide an administrative review queue and test report handling.

Task organisation

The team's Trello board was used to break project work into sprint cards and smaller checklist items. Recorded activities included business research, marketing strategy, proposal preparation and presentation work.

The wider development plan also included project foundations, database design, authentication, listings, browsing, dashboards, reporting, administration, integration testing and deployment.

Individual Reflection — User Stories and Tasks

Working with user stories and tasks helped me understand the importance of approaching a project from the perspective of the people who will eventually use the product. It is easy to focus on the technical aspects of developing a platform, but understanding the actual problem being addressed is what gives those features purpose.

Throughout the UniLinkHub project, I developed a greater appreciation for how an initial idea can be translated into smaller, practical activities. Breaking work down into tasks made the development process easier to understand, organise and monitor. It also highlighted the importance of writing clear requirements so that everyone on the team understands what needs to be achieved.

My involvement in business research, marketing strategy and proposal preparation also helped me see the project beyond its technical requirements. I began to appreciate how the product's purpose, target users and overall business idea influence the features that need to be developed.

This taught me that good development starts with understanding the problem before focusing on the solution. In future projects, I want to become more intentional about translating requirements into clear tasks and ensuring that the work being done continues to serve the needs of the intended users.

Individual Reflection — Product Backlog

The product backlog gave me a broader perspective of the UniLinkHub project and the different stages involved in developing a functional product. Seeing the project organised into larger features and development areas helped me understand how individual tasks contribute to a much bigger objective.

One of the things I learned is that planning is not simply about listing everything that needs to be done. It is also about understanding the relationship between tasks, recognising dependencies and ensuring that the team has a clear direction. A well-maintained backlog makes it easier to identify outstanding work and keep track of the project's overall progress.

I also realised that a backlog should not be treated as a fixed document. As a project develops, requirements can become clearer, priorities can change and new tasks can emerge. This makes continuous review and communication important throughout the Agile process.

Going forward, I would place greater emphasis on keeping backlog items specific, organised and clearly understood by everyone involved. This experience helped me appreciate how effective planning creates a stronger foundation for execution and reduces unnecessary confusion during development.

Individual Reflection — Sprint Backlog

Working with the sprint backlog helped me understand how Agile transforms a large project into smaller, manageable periods of work. Instead of approaching UniLinkHub as one overwhelming development task, the team could focus on specific objectives and monitor progress through individual sprint activities.

Using Trello gave me visibility into how work was distributed, what was currently in progress and which tasks had been completed. I found this particularly useful because it demonstrated the importance of keeping project information organised and accessible to the entire team.

Through my involvement in sprint and backlog support, I developed a better understanding of task coordination and the importance of maintaining accurate progress information. I also recognised that completing individual tasks is only one part of the process; understanding how those tasks contribute to the sprint objective is equally important.

One area I would improve in future projects is consistency in updating task progress and communicating potential challenges earlier. An Agile process works best when everyone has an accurate understanding of what is happening, rather than relying on assumptions about progress.

Overall, this experience strengthened my appreciation for structured execution, teamwork and the discipline required to move a project forward incrementally.

Individual Reflection — Agile Logbook

The Agile Logbook helped me recognise the importance of documenting the development process rather than focusing only on the final product. Throughout a project, there are decisions, task updates, challenges and adjustments that contribute to the final outcome. Keeping a record of these activities makes the development journey easier to understand and evaluate.

Using Trello to monitor cards, assignments and checklist progress demonstrated how project management tools can provide visibility and accountability within a team. It also made me realise that documentation is most useful when it is consistent, accurate and updated as work progresses.

One of my biggest takeaways was that progress should not only be measured by how many tasks have been completed. It is equally important to understand what has been learned, what challenges were encountered and how the team's approach can improve. Reflection creates an opportunity to identify gaps in the process and make better decisions moving forward.

If I were to approach another Agile project, I would be more intentional about documenting my contributions, recording important decisions and maintaining a clearer record of challenges and lessons throughout each sprint. This would make it easier to evaluate my own development and provide meaningful evidence of the work completed.

Ultimately, UniLinkHub showed me that building a product is not only about the technical outcome. It is also about the planning, collaboration, communication and continuous learning that happen along the way.
